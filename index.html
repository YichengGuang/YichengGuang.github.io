About

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the University of Colorado Boulder, advised by Prof. Yueqi Chen. I build the classical machinery of fault-tolerant quantum computers, from the control hardware that drives and reads out the qubits to the decoders that turn syndromes into corrections.

I am also an affiliate of the QubiC team at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, where I lead the development of QubiC 3.0, and I work on quantum error correction decoding with Prof. Xun Gao's group (Physics and JILA, CU Boulder).

Before CU Boulder, I received my B.S. in Computer Science from Nanjing University and spent a semester as an exchange student at the University of Hong Kong.

Interests: quantum control systems · real-time quantum error correction · fault-tolerant quantum architecture

Research

Quantum control systems

Ant-Q: breaking memory bottlenecks in quantum control systems

A memory hierarchy for the QubiC control system on AMD RFSoC boards: on-board DDR4 holds the commands, ping-pong buffers on chip feed each core, and readout streams to the host while the experiment runs. For circuits with at least 1,000 shots, the board time spent outside circuit execution drops from 18.9–104.5% to at most 0.53%. On a transmon at the Advanced Quantum Testbed it ran a 5,101-Clifford randomized-benchmarking sequence and uninterrupted 100 s Ramsey records. Integrated into QubiC's open-source gateware and software.

QubiC 3.0 in progress

The next generation of QubiC. I lead its development, starting with instruction streaming from DDR4 and a readout path to DDR4 in the RISC-Q control SoC.
